We recommend the TF-IDF cosine method because this provides the most recommendations of songs with a matching
genere to the reference song. The dot method had hit count totals of 450 for 0 hits, 64 for 1 hit, and 46 for 2 hits.
The cosine method had hit count totals of 340 for 0 hits, 138 for 1 hit, 55 for 2 hits, and 27 for 3 hits.
Finally, the TF-IDF cosine method had hit count totals of 315 for 0 hits, 152 for 1 hit, 62 for 2 hits, and 31 for 3 hits.
Compared to the raw dot and raw cosine methods, the TF-IDF cosine method does much better and is more accurate because it is taking 
word frequency into account.

One limitation of lyric word counts alone is that the content of the song is not what is being valued as 
much as the length of the song. This is because the raw dot and raw cosine methods do not ignore filler-words.
Instead, those two methods reward them, rather than just focusing on words with substance or words that are less common.

When looking at the IDF dataframe, we noticed that filler words were prominent since we were sorting by IDF score and therefore
document frequency. Even though the TF-IDF cosine method was the best method (compared to the raw dot and raw cosine methods), 
we thought that we could improve it by attempting to remove filler words. We hoped that this would prevent filler words from 
drowning out more desciptive words and therefore provide better recommendations. We tested six different cut-off values 
of document frequency. Since the highest frequency value in the dataframe was around 5,000, we thought that values of 
4,000, 4,500, 3,000, 1,000, 750, and 500 would be the most insightful. We wanted to capture both the extremes of not 
cutting out enough filler words and starting to cut out important words. We found that the optimal cut-off was at 4,000 
since that is when the mean hit count was the highest. Thus, we recommend a TF-IDF cosine method with a document frequency 
cut-off at 4,000 for this dataset. The cut-off should be reevaluated if inputting different data.
